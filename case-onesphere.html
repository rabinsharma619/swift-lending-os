The platform in practice

A migrant-lending programme, end to end

A real lender, a real 43-page requirements document, and a concrete demonstration of what the platform covers — and what deep regulatory research adds before a single line is written.

The situation

The programme

A Malaysian lender intends to serve migrant workers — a segment traditional credit files describe poorly, but whose remittance behaviour is a genuine cash-flow signal.

Two pilot products: Remit-Now-Pay-Later, a loan tied to an outbound remittance, and a short-tenure salary-linked loan. Roughly 146 numbered requirements across 18 series, each with acceptance criteria.

The requirements were reviewed in full against the platform's capabilities — and against the governing statute and the regulator's own published guidance. That second review is where the programme changed.

Why this matters for the platform

Corridor lending is the same mechanic wherever it runs — only the corridor, partner and regulatory pack change. On a configurable platform that makes each new corridor a configuration exercise rather than another build.

Built bespoke, this programme is a one-off. Built on the platform, it is the first deployment of a repeatable product.

What this changes

A programme like this becomes a requirement → capability → gap map rather than a from-scratch build. The work concentrates on what is genuinely specific to this lender — the corridor, the partner integrations and the regulatory pack.

What our research changed

Three findings that reshape the programme

Verified research against the statute and the regulator's own published documents surfaced findings the requirements document did not contain — each one changing the product before it was built.

Finding 1

The target market is at risk

The regulator's published guidance advises that online loans be extended only to citizens and permanent residents — directly adverse to a migrant-worker product.

Advisory rather than statutory, but approval runs through a discretionary committee. This is the first question to put in writing.

Finding 2

The licence sequence is longer than assumed

Only an existing licence holder with more than 90 days of remaining validity may apply for the online endorsement — and the lender holds a conditional approval letter, not an issued licence.

The conventional licence must issue first, then three to six months of processing. Realistically, well over a year.

Finding 3

Agreements need human attestation

The statute requires a qualified person to explain the loan terms and certify the borrower appears to understand them. An unattested agreement is void and unenforceable.

The hardest constraint on a fully remote journey — and the requirements document does not address it anywhere.

And one that decides a product

The governing statute caps unsecured lending interest, prohibits compounding, and makes any additional fee or charge illegal — with sums paid set off against the principal. On a one-month tenure that leaves roughly 1.5% of principal as total revenue, with no fee available to bridge it.

The short-tenure salary-linked product is very unlikely to be viable as specified. The remittance-linked product, at larger tickets and longer tenures, is materially stronger. That is a commercial finding worth more to the client than the software.

In summary

What this programme demonstrates

1 · A real programme, not a hypothesis

A detailed, requirement-ID'd specification from an operating lender — mapped against platform capability line by line.

2 · A project becomes a product

Corridor lending built once and deployed per corridor, rather than rebuilt for each one.

3 · Regulatory depth changes outcomes

The findings above came from primary-source research. Surfacing a product-viability problem before build is worth more than the software that would have implemented it.

4 · The regulatory pack is durable

Bureau reporting, payment rails, identity, statutory documents and supervisory returns are needed regardless. Built once over a market-agnostic kernel, the next market is configuration.

Sequencing note

Because the regulator wants a documented platform and a system security certificate before granting the online endorsement, the first release here is best scoped as a licence-readiness release — the compliance-critical spine, with the commercially rich modules deferred. That is a smaller first build, and it fails cheap if the licence answer is unfavourable.

Where the boundary sits

Platform capability and design patterns are shared across every deployment — that is what makes a platform a platform. A lender's own scoring model, data and configuration are theirs, isolated per deployment, and are never reused across programmes.
