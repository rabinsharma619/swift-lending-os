Framework-native by design

The architecture behind speed and control

Swift Lending OS brings the best of both worlds — the agility of SaaS and the ownership of custom code. Its framework-native design lets lenders assemble, extend and govern lending systems at record speed, without surrendering control.

By combining SDK freedom with prebuilt lending modules, institutions move from design to production in months rather than years — keeping every rule, workflow and integration under their own governance.

Open SDK and APIs

Extend without lock-in. Extend, integrate and customise against documented interfaces. Standard stack, standard patterns, no proprietary runtime to be trapped inside.

Modular Core

Connected workflows, unified data. Origination, servicing, collections and analytics are separate modules over one data model — so the loan a decision created is the loan servicing collects on.

Deploy Anywhere

Your infrastructure, your rules. Cloud, on-premise or hybrid — including fully air-gapped. Every topology runs the same artifacts.

How it is built
What you assemble

Prebuilt modules over one credit kernel

Everything a lender needs to originate, price, book, service, collect and report — connected through a single ledger and a single data model.

THE CREDIT KERNEL

One ledger. One transactional boundary.

Credit operations, balances, schedules, payment allocation and contract types. Every balance change is a discrete, timestamped, reversible, audited operation — no service anywhere writes a balance directly, and any loan's position at any past instant reconstructs from its operation history alone.

01

Origination

Multi-channel intake, deduplication, KYC/AML/fraud orchestration, credit assessment, profile building, offer generation, documents, e-signature and disbursement.

02

Decisioning

Scorecards, decision tables and trees, one expression runtime, a feature store — with dry run, replay, shadow and champion/challenger before anything goes live.

03

Products & pricing

A two-tier product and variant model, pricing scripts reading verified profiles, multiple payment-calculation engines and collateral rules.

04

Servicing

Accrual, charge, payment and past-due operations running automatically; restructures, forbearance, payoff, write-off and general-ledger posting.

05

Payments

Client-level transactions with separate multi-loan allocation, smart and directed allocation, clean rollback, rail and file ingestion, continuous reconciliation.

06

Collections

Segmentation and treatment strategy, contact-compliance enforced by the platform, promise-to-pay, hardship, settlement, charge-off and recoveries.

07

Covenants & monitoring

Metrics, specifications and anchored values — contractual conditions checked continuously for the life of the loan, with breach as a first-class event.

08

Analytics & reporting

Operational views, portfolio and vintage dashboards, regulatory returns and provisioning inputs — generated from the operational record.

Beneath every module

Documents and communications, the connector layer and public API, security, and the audit trio — entity history, activity log and exception log. Nothing happens off the record: every action by a user, job or API is captured with actor, timestamp and before/after state.

How it reshapes

Change the product without changing the system

The line between what is built and what is configured is drawn once, and held.

SDK

Engineers define what is possible

Entities, business processes, events, actions, credit operations, connectors. Ordinary framework code in your repository, reviewed and deployed through your pipeline.

Configuration

The business defines how it behaves

Products and variants, pricing scripts, decision workflows, feature mappings, document and notification templates, roles and queues — versioned, diffable, promotable artifacts.

Operations

Your teams run it

Applications, manual decisions, loan tracking, payments, collections queues, covenant monitoring and dashboards.

The rule that makes this hold

Every configuration option exists because an SDK primitive enables it. A credit policy or price change is a configuration change shipped in hours by the business — not a software release.

Powered by swiftAI

Your AI implementation agent

swiftAI compresses the slowest part of any lending programme — turning business intent into working configuration — without ever touching a credit decision.

Six specialised agents draft products, decision workflows, pricing scripts, tests, migration mappings and operator narratives. Everything they produce is a proposal that enters the same versioned artifact lifecycle as human-authored configuration.

  • It changes the lending system. It never makes a lending decision.
  • Every output is gated — static validation, dry run, replay, shadow, human sign-off.
  • AI-origin artifacts face stricter gates, not looser ones.
  • The platform is fully functional without it — air-gapped deployments run with it absent.

The hard line

There is no code path from any swiftAI component to the decision engine's runtime. The decision of record is always a versioned, deterministic, replayable policy artifact.

That is both a regulatory necessity and the reason the assistance can be trusted anywhere near a credit system.
