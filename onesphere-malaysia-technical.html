Technical paper · revised 22 Nov 2026

OneSphere Malaysia, as an engineering build.

The engineering companion to the business paper: the finOS architecture, the Malaysia solution topology — RNPL, Ria, DuitNow and the KPKT regulatory pack — the delivery timeline and the team. Everything Malaysia-specific is called out.

1 · Architecture overview

finOS — the platform stack.

finOS is a standalone lending platform. OneSphere Malaysia is a client configuration of the RNPL product, which runs on the finOS lending core and its supporting components.

The lending core: the credit kernel, decisioning, the servicing ledger, payments, collections, covenants, and the swappable regulatory packs. The supporting components: identity & access, document management, notifications, enterprise integration, process & task management, the borrower and admin UI, and the AI/ML framework. Both are part of finOS and follow the corridor SNPL design — built for OneSphere first and then reused by the corridors, not a Malaysia-only fork.

3 · The credit kernel

One transactional boundary, one ledger.

The correctness core is a single transactional boundary — one PostgreSQL, ACID. A balance is only ever the sum of credit_operation entries against it (decision D4); there is no "update balance". External rails and partners interact through SAGA at the edges only — never inside the kernel.

Why it matters for the licence

This is what makes the KPKT licence defensible: the 18% cap, the no-compounding rule and the fee prohibition are enforced by the Guardrail Service outside policy scripts, and any decision or balance replays exactly from stored inputs, years later.

4 · Solution topology

The RNPL request, end to end.

A single RNPL request, end-to-end. The remittance is disbursed by Ria as remitter of record (keeping OneSphere out of money transmission); repayment is collected by DuitNow AutoDebit and lands as a PAYMENT operation allocated by the servicing waterfall.

Malaysia-specific bindings

Regulatory pack: regpack-malaysia (KPKT) — the 18% cap, s.23 fee prohibition, s.27 attestation gate, and the citizenship guardrail, all enforced outside policy scripts.
Rails: ria-edge (remittance disbursement) + duitnow-edge (repayment). No connectIPS/NCHL.
Currency: loan in MYR; remittance value in NPR (FX handled at the Ria edge, not in the ledger).
Data residency: the data plane is hosted in Malaysia for PDPA; Kathmandu engineers work against anonymised/masked non-production data.

6 · Deployment & runtime

A dedicated deployment, hosted by Swift in Malaysia.

OneSphere runs as a dedicated finOS deployment, hosted by Swift on Kubernetes in a Malaysian region (ADR-24). The code is multi-tenant-ready from day one (tenant_id + row-level security), but OneSphere shares its deployment with no other lender. Default-deny between namespaces; only integration has internet egress (per-provider allow-list); the data plane is hosted in Malaysia for PDPA.

6.2 · Event bus & the transactional outbox

lending-core writes domain events to an outbox_event table in the same transaction as the state change; a publisher drains it to signed webhooks by default or, where enabled, Kafka (ADR-10), keyed by aggregate id for ordering. Nothing is lost, nothing double-posts. Batch work (accrual, past-due, reports) is scheduled from PostgreSQL (ShedLock), so it never depends on the bus.

7 · Regulatory-pack architecture

Statutory limits enforced by the platform, not by configuration.

The core stays market-agnostic; every jurisdiction's rules live in a versioned, effective-dated pack. OneSphere Malaysia adds regpack-malaysia alongside the planned NRB, SEBON, and FCA packs — a new pack, not a fork of the core. Guardrails execute in a Guardrail SPI outside the policy scripts business users edit (D10), so a configuration mistake cannot breach a statutory limit.

regpack-malaysia encodes

  • Interest cap — 18% p.a. unsecured / 12% secured (s.17A) as a hard RATE_CEILING clamp; breach is impossible to persist.
  • Fee prohibition — s.23: no arrangement/platform/service fees; the product schema forbids them.
  • No compounding — structurally guaranteed by the fixed-schedule ledger.
  • Attestation gate — s.27: an application cannot reach DISBURSING until the attestation step is evidenced (a required, audited condition).
  • Borrower-eligibility rule — the citizenship/permit-status check as a PROHIBITION keyed to the KPKT position confirmed in Phase 0.
  • Reporting — the KPKT online-lending returns generated from the reporting mart.

8 · Delivery timeline

Gated by the licence, not by engineering.

The go-live is gated by the KPKT licence track, not by engineering. Phase 0 has not started; months count from its start (T0, date not yet set), and Swift staffs the build from its in-house engineering team. Engineering can be pilot-ready around month 8; the live date waits on full online approval. The conventional licence is issued (2026-11-20), before T0; what remains is conditional → build → full, with the online application lodged during Phase 0.

The mix, and why it matters here

  • Kernel/domain correctness is the highest bar — the ledger's property-test suite is ~half of the credit module's effort; a defect here is a licence problem, not a bug.
  • Regulatory is a standing function with commit rights, not advisory — the Malaysia pack (D10) must be authored and reviewed by the regulatory lead + counsel, not guessed by engineers.
  • Estate/partner liaison (Ria, DuitNow, KYC) is heavier than pure build — much of Rails & Connectors is integration and certification, not coding.
  • Two non-negotiable hires from day zero: a credit-operations domain expert and a Malaysia regulatory lead. "Every failed lending platform was built by engineers guessing at what an accrual convention — or a rate cap — is."
