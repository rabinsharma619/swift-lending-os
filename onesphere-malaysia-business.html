Business paper · revised 22 Nov 2026

Launching OneSphere's lending business in Malaysia.

A remittance-linked lending business, built by Swift on finOS, under a KPKT online-moneylending approval — the corridor SNPL design, built for OneSphere first and then reused by the corridors.

1 · The decision requested

Approve a phased, RNPL-first launch — starting with Phase 0.

Approve a phased, RNPL-first launch of OneSphere's Malaysia lending business, built by Swift on finOS and operated under a KPKT online-moneylending approval. Concretely, three decisions:

01

Start a bounded Phase 0 (13 weeks), staffed by Swift's in-house team, to close the four make-or-break questions in §7 and baseline the OS-R1 PRD — before scope is fixed. Phase 0 has not started yet, and OneSphere is Swift's only active build until the pilot gate (OS-G1).

02

Adopt RNPL (Remit-Now-Pay-Later) as the launch product, and defer the Pay Day Loan until its unit economics are proven under the interest-rate cap.

03

Build on finOS, following the corridor SNPL design — built for OneSphere first and then reused by the corridors — rather than commissioning a greenfield lending stack.

This is not a request to approve a multi-quarter build up front. The launch is gated by a licence and four legal questions, not by engineering velocity — so the paper asks for a gate-by-gate commitment, with the first gate being small. Swift builds finOS and the RNPL solution with its own in-house engineers, so no gate is a funding request.

2 · Executive summary

A real opportunity, gated by answerable questions.

The opportunity is real and specific

Hundreds of thousands of Nepali migrant workers in Malaysia are thin-file, income-earning, and remittance-dependent — underserved by conventional credit because they have no local bureau footprint. Their remittance behaviour is a verifiable, proprietary cash-flow signal. OneSphere, a licensed moneylender with Ria as its remittance partner, is unusually well placed to lend against it.

The vehicle is designed

OneSphere's launch product — RNPL — is the same mechanic as Swift's SNPL (Send-Now-Pay-Later) designed for the UK→Nepal and Korea→Nepal corridors, with the same partner. Built on finOS, the credit kernel, decisioning, servicing ledger and collections — and the supporting components (identity, documents, notifications, integration, borrower and admin UI, and native accounting) — follow the corridor SNPL design: built for OneSphere first and then reused by the corridors, not a Malaysia-only build.

The gating is regulatory, and it is honest work

Three findings shape the entire plan, and none of them is an engineering problem:

  • KPKT advises that online loans be given only to Malaysian citizens and permanent residents (FAQ Q5). This is advisory, not statutory — but it points directly at the migrant target market, and approval runs through a discretionary committee. This one question must be put to KPKT in writing before the product is designed. Nothing else matters if the answer is no.
  • Every loan agreement requires human attestation under s.27 of the Moneylenders Act 1951; an unattested agreement is void. This is the hardest constraint on a fully remote journey, and the BRD is silent on it.
  • The interest-rate cap (18% p.a. unsecured) makes the Pay Day Loan look unviable — a RM500 / 30-day advance earns ~RM7.40 gross, which does not cover its eKYC check. RNPL at larger tickets and longer tenures (RM2,000 / 6 months ≈ RM180 gross at a flat rate, ≈ RM106 on a reducing balance) is the workable product.

The recommendation

Proceed — RNPL-first, on finOS — and treat the four questions in §7 as the plan's spine, not its footnotes. A launch paper that hid them would be worthless; a launch that resolves them is defensible, differentiated, and builds the corridor SNPL design once — for OneSphere first, then reused by the corridors.

Evidence discipline. Every regulatory figure and product parameter here is drawn from the primary-source reviews already in this repository — the KPKT licence-readiness strategy (reviews/ekpkt-licence-readiness-strategy.md), the OneSphere BRD review and critique (reviews/onesphere-malaysia-brd-*.md), and the finOS architecture (architecture/, architecture diagram/standalone-architecture.md). Where a number is a regulator's stated service standard rather than an observed one, or a legal position that still needs counsel, it is flagged. This paper does not invent figures to fill gaps.

3 · The opportunity

A creditworthy market, invisible to conventional credit.

The customer

Nepali migrant workers in Malaysia: documented work-permit holders, earning a wage, sending money home on a regular cadence. They are thin-file by definition — no CCRIS/CTOS history, no local credit relationship — and therefore excluded from mainstream credit despite being creditworthy on a cash-flow basis.

The signal

Remittance regularity, consistency, and amount-stability are a cash-flow proxy no generic lender has. OneSphere sees it natively through Ria. A borrower who has remitted a stable sum every month for a year is demonstrating exactly the behaviour a lender wants to underwrite.

The product with the edge — RNPL

Remit-Now-Pay-Later advances the remittance and collects it back over 1–6 months. It is:

  • Purpose-linked and verifiable — proceeds fund a real, observed transaction (a remittance to family), not unrestricted cash. That is strong purpose control and defensible regulatory optics.
  • Differentiated — it is a proprietary signal on a proprietary rail, not a commodity loan.
  • A corridor, not a one-off — the identical mechanic is designed for UK→Nepal and Korea→Nepal. Malaysia is the first build of one corridor product design, which those corridors then reuse.
Market direction of travel. The migrant-lending opportunity is real but ethically and regulatorily sensitive (ILO/IOM flag migrant over-indebtedness). RNPL's purpose-linkage is exactly the structure that survives that scrutiny; an unrestricted payday product is exactly the structure that invites it. The opportunity and the recommended product shape point the same way.

RNPL first. Pay Day Loan deferred, deliberately.

Unit-economics note (recommendation). The Pay Day Loan is rejected below at RM 7.40 a loan, yet RNPL's own floor (RM 300 × 1 month) earns RM 4.44, and the RM 180 headline assumes flat interest. Until KPKT confirms how the cap is computed (§7, question 3), the platform applies it in the most conservative way (to the effective rate), which brings a flat-priced loan's yield close to the reducing-balance figure. Recommendation: raise the pilot floor to RM 1,000 and 3 months, and at OS-G0 recompute every ticket in the range on KPKT's confirmed method against the per-loan cost to serve (eKYC, screening, bureau, AutoDebit, attestation, Ria). Worked figures are in the OS-R1 PRD §3.1. The bounds are product parameters; OneSphere decides.

RemitScore blends remittance consistency, remittance-relationship depth, behavioural reliability, an affordability cushion, and (over time) prior OneSphere repayment. It is deliberately rule-based and reproducible at launch, not a black box — decisions must replay from stored inputs and carry reason codes, which the licence and responsible-lending posture both require.

  • The remittance signal is partly endogenous. RNPL lends to fund remittance, and the score is driven by remittance history. A borrower remitting out of income and one remitting on OneSphere credit look similar to the model but are different risks. The pilot must flag debt-funded remittance and test whether the signal predicts repayment before the weights are trusted.
  • Affordability shares a root with the remittance signal (income is inferred from remittance). Treated as independent inputs they will over-separate scores. The model must not double-count.

4.2 · Pay Day Loan — deferred

The BRD proposes a parallel Pay Day Loan (RM 200–1,500, 14–45 days, unrestricted cash). The evidence says defer it:

  • Unit economics fail at the cap. At 18% p.a., a RM500 / 30-day advance earns ~RM7.40 gross — below the cost of the eKYC check alone. Fees cannot bridge the gap: s.23 allows only the charges the Act permits, and any other sum paid is set off against the principal (the permitted list is to be confirmed with counsel).
  • Worst regulatory optics in consumer credit, into a brand-new CCA 2025 regime that invites scrutiny, with documented debt-spiral risk and no rollover cap in the BRD.
  • It confounds the pilot — its risk profile is nothing like RNPL's, so launching both together muddies the learnings and attaches payday's liability to RNPL's launch.

Deferring it is not abandoning it: it re-enters scope only if the flat-vs-reducing interest computation and a defensible fee position make the numbers work.

5 · The platform

finOS — designed for the corridors, built for OneSphere first.

5.1 · What finOS provides

finOS is a standalone lending platform. For this launch it splits into three parts:

  • Supporting components: identity & access, reference data, document management, notifications, enterprise integration, process & task management, and the borrower and admin UI — part of finOS, shared across every corridor. (Swift Intelligence helps Swift's engineers build at design time only; no AI runs in a credit decision, D9.)
  • Lending core: the credit kernel, decisioning, servicing ledger, payments, collections, the jurisdiction/regulatory packs, and native accounting (general ledger, cash and bank, financial statements — ADR-30), which OneSphere needs because it has no accounting system of its own (Q67).
  • RNPL for OneSphere Malaysia: the product configuration, the client release, and its Malaysia pack.

5.2 · The reuse map — cheaper and more credible than greenfield

RNPL is the same design as SNPL, designed for two corridors with the same partner and built for OneSphere first. The honest vendor position is "requirement → finOS capability (built for OneSphere first) → Malaysia-specific gap," not a greenfield quote:

  • Onboarding, KYC, documents, notifications, integration, multi-language UI — Swift finOS components, built for OneSphere first and then reused by the corridors.
  • The credit kernel, RemitScore decisioning, the servicing ledger, and collections — the lending core, shared with the corridors.
  • The Malaysia/KPKT regulatory pack is a new, swappable pack alongside the planned NRB, SEBON, and FCA packs — not a fork of the core.

5.3 · The platform advantages that matter here

  • Regulatory guardrails execute outside policy scripts (D10). The 18% p.a. cap, the no-compounding rule, and the fee restriction are enforced by the platform and cannot be overridden by a configuration mistake — the single most important control for a moneylending licence.
  • Every decision replays exactly, years later — pinned policy versions + stored inputs. This is regulator-facing evidence, not a promise.
  • Policy is versioned like software — RemitScore weights, templates, and pricing are versioned, effective-dated artifacts; the pilot's re-weighting is a controlled release, not a code change.
  • Swift Intelligence compresses configuration — it drafts config and tests as proposals a human approves; it never touches a credit decision.

6 · The regulatory path — KPKT online moneylending

An endorsement on a moneylending licence — not an “eKPKT licence”.

There is no "eKPKT licence." What OneSphere needs is approval by the Registrar of Moneylenders to conduct online moneylending — an additional condition endorsed on an existing Act 400 (PPW) moneylending licence. As of the KPKT register, ~79 companies hold this endorsement; all carry an ordinary WL####/##/## licence number. Getting the terminology right matters in every filing.

    premises + police screening cleared; >90 days validity

  2. Step 2

    Conditional approval

    documented customer journey + system security certificate

  3. Step 3

    Full approval

Update 2026-11-20: OneSphere's KPKT moneylending licence is issued — step 1 is complete. The online application can now be lodged, with the finOS system plan as its documented customer journey and technology description; the stated 3–6-month processing standard runs from a complete application, and backlog remains a risk. More than 90 days of licence validity must remain when it is lodged. (Open: whether the conditional tier still operates in 2026, or was a 2020–21 pioneer mechanism — this decides whether we build before or after applying.)

6.4 · CCA 2025 — not a live deadline

The Consumer Credit Act 2025 (Act 873, in force 1 March 2026) is not a live deadline for OneSphere. Phase 1 regulates six previously unregulated activities (BNPL, factoring, leasing, debt collection, impaired-loan acquisition, debt counselling); moneylending under Act 400 is expressly excluded (s.79(1)(b)) and stays with KPKT. Migration of moneylending into the new regime is Phase 2 (~2028–2030) — plan for it, don't gate on it. One thing does apply now: CCA Phase 1 expands KPKT's remit to Islamic financing and Ar-Rahnu, so an Islamic product line is a live, KPKT-regulated option (see §8).

6.5 · Data residency

Swift's engineering is in Kathmandu; Malaysia has cross-border data-transfer and PDPA obligations. Hosting is decided: OneSphere runs as a dedicated finOS deployment, hosted by Swift in a Malaysian region (ADR-24). Still open, and assessed in Phase 0: whether Swift engineers in Kathmandu may access production data, and whether production-like UAT data is permissible.

7 · The four questions that decide the business

Ordered by “nothing downstream matters until this is answered.”

These are the make-or-break inputs. Phase 0 exists to close them. They are ordered by "nothing downstream matters until this is answered."

01

Citizenship (ask KPKT in writing, immediately)

Will KPKT approve online lending to documented work-permit holders? If the answer is a firm no, the migrant thesis needs a citizen/PR segment or a different structure — and everything else waits.

02

Attestation (s.27) in an online journey

How is human attestation satisfied remotely? Ask KPKT; reverse-engineer from an approved licensee's published Schedule J. This shapes the entire UX before it is designed.

03

Rate-cap unit economics

Confirm whether 12%/18% is computed flat or on reducing balance — it materially changes yield — and confirm RNPL's economics and Pay Day Loan's non-viability.

04

MSB exposure on the remittance leg

Structure so Ria is remitter of record and OneSphere merely lends; if OneSphere holds, nets, or transmits customer funds cross-border, a Money Services Business Act 2011 licence is likely in play. KPKT monitors online lenders with BNM — assume BNM interest.

(Second-tier, before scope freeze: data residency; the security-certificate definition — digital CA vs security-assurance report; whether the conditional tier still runs; a system-enforced product sandbox gate; and the migrant-specific credit controls in §9.)

8 · Islamic option · Malaysia-specific, optional

A Shariah-compliant variant is available — demand-led, never a rate-cap workaround.

Because CCA Phase 1 brings Islamic moneylending and Ar-Rahnu under KPKT, a Shariah-compliant RNPL variant is available — and the finOS design models Islamic finance as a contract type in the kernel (fixed sale price, profit recognised not accrued; a first-class charity-payable ledger for late charges), with a Malaysia/BNM Shariah pack distinct from the Gulf/AAOIFI pack.

Two cautions govern any Islamic move:

  • It must be demand-led, not a rate-cap workaround. Using an Islamic wrapper to escape the 18% ceiling would be hilah (impermissible artifice) and would draw equal regulatory hostility.
  • Counsel must confirm whether the s.17A cap applies to an Islamic profit rate, whether separate prescribed agreement forms exist, and whether a Shariah Committee is mandatory for a KPKT Islamic moneylender.

Recommendation

Conventional RNPL first; scope an Islamic variant only if a genuine market wants it and the questions above are answered.

11 · How we will know it worked

Five tests, set before the pilot starts.

✓

The four §7 questions are answered and the migrant segment is confirmed approvable.

✓

KPKT full online approval is granted on the RNPL customer journey.

✓

The pilot validates the remittance-signal thesis on real repayment — approval rate, first-payment default, 7+/30+ DPD by month on book, and score-band separation within pre-set bounds, each reported with its confidence — or the stop criteria halt it cleanly.

✓

OneSphere passes inspection from the first loan — AML/CFT, complaints, statements, write-off and hardship controls are live before the pilot, not after it.

✓

RNPL unit economics hold at the rate cap; the platform's guardrails demonstrably prevent a cap or fee breach; every decision replays for a regulator.

12 · Recommendation

Proceed — RNPL-first, on finOS. Start Phase 0 now.

The opportunity is genuine and OneSphere is uniquely placed to serve it. The vehicle is designed: RNPL is the corridor SNPL design on the same finOS core, built for OneSphere first and then reused by the corridors, so the shared design and the time-to-market advantage are real. The constraints are equally real — the citizenship advisory, the s.27 attestation, the rate cap, and the MSB question decide the business — but they are answerable, and the plan is built around answering them first, cheaply, before any scope is frozen.

Do not launch the Pay Day Loan into a rate cap that makes it unviable and a new regime that invites scrutiny. Do launch RNPL — a differentiated, purpose-linked product on a proprietary signal — as a pilot that tests its thesis, on a platform whose guardrails make the licence defensible and whose shared design makes the launch fast.
