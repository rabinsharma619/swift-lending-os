Executive summary

The opportunity is real and specific

Hundreds of thousands of Nepali migrant workers in Malaysia are thin-file, income-earning, and remittance-dependent — underserved because they have no local bureau footprint. Their remittance behaviour is a verifiable, proprietary cash-flow signal. OneSphere, as IME Group's arm with Ria as partner, is unusually well placed to lend against it.

The vehicle already exists

The launch product is the same mechanic as Swift's SNPL for the UK→Nepal and Korea→Nepal corridors, same partner. Built as a Domain on EDX, ~10 enterprise services — identity, documents, notifications, integration, metadata UI, MLOps — are reused, not rebuilt. Swift builds the lending Domain, not a platform.

The gating is regulatory — and honest work

Three findings shape the whole plan: KPKT advises lending only to citizens/PRs (points at the migrant market); every agreement needs human attestation under s.27; and the 18% rate cap makes the Pay Day Loan unviable while SNPL works.

The recommendation

Proceed — SNPL-first, on Swift LendingOS on EDX — and treat the four questions as the plan's spine, not its footnotes. A launch that resolves them is defensible, differentiated, and reuses machinery already largely built.

RemitScore blends remittance consistency & relationship depth, behavioural reliability, and an affordability cushion — rule-based and reproducible at launch, with reason codes, not a black box.

Two cautions the pilot must resolve

  • The remittance signal is partly endogenous. SNPL lends to fund remittance; a borrower remitting out of income and one remitting on credit look alike but are different risks. The pilot must flag debt-funded remittance and prove the signal predicts repayment before the weights are trusted.
  • Affordability shares a root with the signal (income inferred from remittance). Treated as independent inputs, scores over-separate. The model must not double-count.

Pay Day Loan — deferred

At 18% p.a., a RM500 / 30-day advance earns ~RM 7.40 gross — below its own eKYC cost — and s.23 makes fee workarounds illegal. Worst regulatory optics into a new regime; confounds the pilot. It re-enters scope only if the numbers work.

Why on EDX, not standalone

For a time-pressured new-market launch, EDX-on-top reuses ~10 platform services, ships one metadata-driven codebase for Flutter + web, integrates natively with Ria and the rails, and inherits mature, regulated-production controls. The control that matters for a licence

Regulatory guardrails execute outside policy scripts. The 18% cap, the no-compounding rule and the fee prohibition are enforced by the platform and cannot be overridden by a configuration mistake — and every decision replays exactly, years later, as regulator-facing evidence.

The regulatory path — KPKT online moneylending

An endorsement on a moneylending licence — not an "eKPKT licence".

There is no separate "eKPKT licence." OneSphere needs the Registrar's approval to conduct online moneylending — an additional condition endorsed on an existing Act 400 (PPW) licence. ~79 companies hold it today; all carry an ordinary WL####/##/## number.

CCA 2025 — not a live deadline

Act 873 (in force 1 Mar 2026) regulates six other activities; moneylending stays with KPKT (s.79(1)(b)). Migration is Phase 2 (~2028) — plan for it, don't gate on it. Phase 1 does expand KPKT's remit to Islamic moneylending & Ar-Rahnu.

Data residency

Swift's engineering is in Kathmandu; Malaysia has cross-border + PDPA obligations. This decides hosting, production-data access, and UAT data — assess before build.

The four questions that decide the business

Ordered by "nothing downstream matters until this is answered."

Phase 0 exists to close them.

01 · Citizenship — ask KPKT in writing, now

Will KPKT approve online lending to documented work-permit holders? A firm "no" reshapes the entire thesis. Nothing else matters until this is answered.

02 · Attestation (s.27) online

How is human attestation satisfied remotely? Ask KPKT; reverse-engineer from an approved licensee's Schedule J. Shapes the entire UX before it is designed.

03 · Rate-cap unit economics

Is 12%/18% computed flat or on reducing balance? It materially changes yield — and confirms SNPL's economics and the Pay Day Loan's non-viability.

04 · MSB exposure on the remittance leg

Structure so Ria is remitter of record and OneSphere merely lends. If OneSphere holds/nets/transmits funds cross-border, a Money Services Business licence is likely in play.

Recommendation

Proceed — SNPL-first, on Swift LendingOS on EDX. Fund Phase 0 now.

The opportunity is genuine and OneSphere is uniquely placed to serve it. The vehicle is largely built — the launch product is the corridor SNPL, and Swift LendingOS is the Domain EDX already names — so the reuse and the time-to-market advantage are real.

The constraints are equally real — the citizenship advisory, the s.27 attestation, the rate cap, and the MSB question decide the business — but they are answerable, and the plan is built around answering them first, cheaply, before any scope is frozen.

Don't launch a Pay Day Loan into a rate cap that makes it unviable. Do launch SNPL — a differentiated, purpose-linked product on a proprietary signal — as a pilot that tests its thesis, on a platform whose guardrails make the licence defensible and whose reuse makes the launch fast.
